South Africa

ZAF
Today
  1. South Africa's unemployment hit 31.88%, a crisis that no growth forecast can fix without structural change.
  2. The IMF cut emerging market growth and raised inflation fears, hitting South Africa harder than most because the country's 1.5% growth forecast barely beats population growth.
  3. High debt and fading aid squeeze South Africa's ability to spend on jobs, trapping the country in a slow-growth trap.

GDP Growth

+1.5%
  1. South Africa's economy is growing at only 1.5%, which means the country is barely creating enough new jobs and income to keep up with population growth.
  2. This growth rate is half the speed of sub-Saharan Africa overall, signaling that South Africa is losing economic momentum while its neighbors recover faster.
  3. At 1.5% growth, South Africa cannot meaningfully reduce its 32.5% unemployment rate without structural reforms to productivity and investment.
GDP Growth · Annual
What is GDP growth?

GDP growth measures how fast a country's economy is expanding or shrinking. It is the single most important number for understanding whether an economy is healthy, struggling, or in recession.

Source: National statistics offices · IMF

Inflation (CPI)

3.5%
  1. Inflation is at 3.5%, which is actually lower than you might expect given that global food and energy prices are rising, so South Africa's central bank has room to cut rates.
  2. This low inflation is unusual because sub-Saharan Africa's median is supposed to jump from 3.4% to 5%, meaning South Africa is anchoring expectations better than its peers.
  3. The 3.5% number tells you that South Africa's rand weakness and import costs have not yet triggered a wage-price spiral that would force the central bank to keep rates high.
Inflation (CPI) · History since 2000
What is inflation?

Inflation is a sustained increase in the overall price level of goods and services, which reduces the purchasing power of money over time.

Source: National statistics offices · IMF

Unemployment

32.5%
  1. 32.5% unemployment means roughly one in three working-age South Africans cannot find a job, making this the single largest drag on the country's development and household incomes.
  2. This rate has barely moved in five years despite multiple policy reforms, which tells you that job creation requires something more fundamental than the current approach is delivering.
  3. At 1.5% growth, unemployment will likely tick higher over the next 12 months unless the government can unlock private investment in labor-intensive sectors like construction and manufacturing.
What is the unemployment rate?

The unemployment rate measures what percentage of people who want to work cannot find a job. It is one of the clearest signals of whether an economy is providing enough opportunity for its citizens.

Source: National statistics offices · IMF

Budget Deficit

-4.8% GDP
  1. The government is spending 4.8% more than it collects in taxes, which means it borrows money to fund spending, and when governments borrow too much, they crowd out private businesses that also want to borrow.
  2. At a 4.8% deficit, South Africa is spending faster than its 1.5% growth rate can sustain, which means the debt burden will keep rising and eventually force painful spending cuts.
  3. The high budget deficit is why the 10-year bond yield is 9.05%, because lenders demand higher interest rates to compensate for the risk that the government cannot pay back what it owes.
Budget Deficit · Annual
What is the budget deficit?

The budget deficit measures how much more a government spends than it earns in a given year. A deficit means the government is borrowing to cover the gap, adding to national debt.

Source: IMF Fiscal Monitor · National finance ministries

Current Account

-1.4% GDP
  1. South Africa imports more goods and services than it exports, running a 1.4% deficit, which means foreign currency is flowing out faster than it is flowing in.
  2. A current account deficit forces the country to borrow from abroad or sell assets to foreigners, and the weaker rand at 16.56 per dollar shows that the market is pricing in continued pressure.
  3. If the deficit widens much beyond 1.4%, the rand will weaken further, making imports more expensive and pushing inflation higher, which would force the central bank to raise rates again.
Current Account · Annual
What is the current account?

The current account measures whether a country earns more from the rest of the world than it spends. It tracks trade in goods and services, plus income flows like investment returns and remittances.

Source: IMF BOP Statistics

Policy Rate

6.25%
  1. The central bank has set interest rates at 6.25%, which is high enough to discourage borrowing and slow the economy, but necessary to prevent inflation from accelerating.
  2. With inflation at only 3.5%, the real (inflation-adjusted) interest rate is 2.75%, which is quite high and makes it expensive for businesses to borrow money to invest and hire workers.
  3. If the central bank cuts rates in the next 12 months, borrowing becomes cheaper and growth could accelerate, but inflation could also rise, so the bank has to be careful.
Policy Rate · History since 2000
What is the policy rate?

The policy rate is the interest rate set by a country's central bank. It is the most powerful tool for controlling inflation and influencing economic growth, because it affects the cost of borrowing for everyone.

Source: National central banks

Stock Market YTD

+2.8%
  1. South Africa's stock market gained 2.8% year-to-date, a modest climb that reflects investor caution as the IMF cuts growth forecasts and inflation risks rise.
  2. The market is treading water because the economy faces headwinds: slower growth, higher inflation expectations, and tighter global financial conditions all squeeze corporate earnings.
  3. Watch whether the market can hold above 3% by mid-year, because a breakdown signals that investors are losing confidence in the economic recovery narrative.
Stock Market YTD · History since 2000
What is the stock market YTD?

Stock Market YTD shows how much a country's main stock market index has gone up or down since the start of the year. It is the most visible daily scoreboard for investor confidence in a country's economy and companies.

Source: Exchange data

10Y Bond Yield

9.05%
  1. South African government bonds pay 9.05%, which is steep but reflects genuine worry that the government might struggle with its debt load.
  2. Investors demand this high payout as insurance against the possibility that spending cuts don't happen fast enough to stabilize finances.
  3. If the BRICS group influence grows as analysts suggest, demand for ZA bonds could improve, which would push yields down and bond prices up.
10Y Bond Yield · History since 2000
What is the 10-year bond yield?

The 10-year bond yield is the annual return investors earn for lending money to a government for 10 years. It is the most important interest rate in any economy, the benchmark that influences mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs, and stock valuations.

Source: Bloomberg · Refinitiv

Yield Curve

+230bps
  1. The difference between short-term and long-term South African borrowing costs is 230 basis points, which is unusually steep and tells you that investors are nervous about what happens next year.
  2. When the curve gets this fat, it usually means people expect interest rates to fall or the economy to improve, but in this case it mostly reflects fear that near-term fiscal strain requires painful cuts.
  3. A curve this steep is actually good news for banks, which borrow short and lend long, so financial stocks should benefit if this shape holds.
Yield Curve · History since 2000
What is the yield curve?

The yield curve shows the difference between short-term and long-term government bond yields. Its shape is one of the most reliable predictors of recessions: when long-term rates fall below short-term rates (an inversion), a recession has followed almost every time in history.

Source: Bloomberg · Refinitiv

USD/ZAR

16.30
  1. The rand trades at 16.80 per dollar, about 1.97% weaker than where it started the year, meaning South African goods and services became cheaper for foreign buyers but imports cost more for local consumers.
  2. Currency weakness hurts because South Africa imports energy and food, so a weaker rand makes inflation stickier even as cheap Chinese solar panels arrive to help with the power crisis.
  3. Watch whether the rand stabilizes here or keeps sliding, because if it breaks above 17.50, that inflation problem gets worse and the Reserve Bank might have to keep rates high longer.
USD/ZAR · History since 2000
South Africa's FX regime: Free Float

South Africa's rand floats freely — its value is determined by markets. The South African Reserve Bank (SARB) does not target a specific exchange rate, though it may intervene to smooth extreme volatility. USD/ZAR shows how many rand you need to buy one US dollar. At 17.8, one dollar costs about 18 rand. The rand is volatile because South Africa depends on commodity exports and foreign capital inflows — when global investors get nervous, the rand often falls sharply.

What is the exchange rate?

The exchange rate is how much one currency is worth in terms of another. It determines the price of everything a country imports and exports, and is one of the most visible daily indicators of a country's economic standing in the world.

Source: WM/Refinitiv · Bloomberg · National central banks
