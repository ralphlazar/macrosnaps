# MacroSnaps Marketing Brief
_Last updated: March 18, 2026_

---

## Positioning

Do not compete with Bloomberg or data terminals. The competition is a good morning read, not a data subscription. The audience is professionals and informed non-professionals who want a daily briefing at the depth they choose.

The three-tier depth selector (beginner / moderate / expert) is the key differentiator. No competitor surfaces the same data at three levels of explanation simultaneously. Lead with this in all content.

---

## Target Audience

**Primary:** Finance, consulting, policy, and journalism professionals who want a credible daily brief without data terminal overhead.

**Secondary:** Engaged general readers, students, and economics-curious people who find the FT too slow and Reuters too raw.

## Channels

### X / Twitter
- Account: @ralphlazar (personal). Register @macrosnaps to protect the handle but leave it dormant.
- People follow people on X, not products. Post as Ralph.
- Post daily. Multiple times a day is fine — X does not penalise frequency.
- **Morning:** 3-4 tweet thread. Flags, sharp contrast or observation, link in the thread (no penalty for links on X).
- **Afternoon:** Single take reacting to a market event as it unfolds. No link needed.
- Grow from zero by replying to bigger macro accounts with genuinely useful insight. People click through to profiles of accounts that say smart things.
- Accounts worth engaging: @Robin_Brooks, @elerianm, @Frances_Coppola, @NickTimiraos.
- Post the morning thread between 7-9am GMT — that is when the target audience is scanning feeds.
- Tone: strong takes, not engagement bait.

### LinkedIn
- Post from your personal profile, not the MacroSnaps company page. Personal profiles get far more algorithmic reach.
- Create a MacroSnaps company page for brand presence, but publish all content as Ralph.
- 3-4 times a week maximum. Never more than once a day — a second post the same day competes with the first.
- **Post structure:** Sharp hook on line 1 (a claim or contrast, not a fact dump). 2-3 short paragraphs of insight. Question at the end to drive comments.
- Always put the macrosnaps.app link in the first comment, never the post body. LinkedIn suppresses posts with outbound links.
- Comments are the single biggest amplification signal on LinkedIn. A question at the end ("Which central bank gets the call right — hike, hold, or cut?") pushes the post into commenters' networks.
- Tone: moderate-tier register. Informed but not jargon-heavy.

### Email Newsletter
- Weekly digest of the three global stories. Low effort since the app generates them daily.
- Recommended starting platform: Substack. Free forever for free newsletters, with built-in discovery.
- Buttondown is cleaner and more developer-friendly: free up to 100 subscribers, then $9/month for up to 1,000. No revenue cut.
- Beehiiv: free up to 2,500 subscribers but heavier than needed at launch.
- Add a one-line CTA with a Substack link to the macrosnaps.app footer: e.g. "Get the weekly macro brief — free in your inbox."

### Reddit
- Subreddits: r/investing, r/MacroEconomics, r/Economics.
- Use a personal or neutral pseudonym — not u/macrosnaps and not your real name. A recognisable but non-corporate handle avoids every comment reading as marketing.
- Answer questions with genuine depth. Mention MacroSnaps only when it is the honest answer to someone's question.
- Never directly promote. Reddit users are allergic to self-promotion but receptive to genuine expertise.

### Product Hunt
- Do not launch immediately. Wait until the pipeline has run cleanly for at least 2-3 weeks and the site has 10-20 genuine early users who can leave authentic comments on launch day.
- Prepare a short demo video or GIF before submitting — listings with visuals perform significantly better.
- Treat Product Hunt as a public coming-out moment, not a first step.
- Sequencing: X and LinkedIn first, email list running, stable operation for a few weeks, then Product Hunt.

### Niche Newsletter Pitches
- Pitch a short write-up of MacroSnaps to newsletters covering fintech tools and data products.
- Targets: Ben's Bites, TLDR Finance, The Rundown.
- One mention in the right newsletter outperforms weeks of social posting for bringing in relevant users.

### Academic Outreach
- Email 20-30 macro economics lecturers at good universities directly and honestly — not using the "asking for advice" pretence.
- Frame it genuinely: "I built a daily macro dashboard aimed at making G7 and BRICS data accessible at different levels of expertise. I think it could be useful for students or as a teaching reference. I would love your feedback."
- One lecturer who mentions MacroSnaps to 200 students a year is worth a lot. Course page links also carry credibility that social posting cannot buy.
- Keep it targeted and high quality, not a mass email campaign.

### TikTok
- Not worth it right now as a solo founder.
- Too much production effort for the wrong audience at this stage.
- FinTok is a real niche but requires daily video production — not viable alongside running the pipeline solo.
- Revisit if and when there is someone helping with content.

## LinkedIn Post Formula

1. Line 1: a claim or surprising contrast — not a fact dump
2. Lines 2-4: 2-3 short paragraphs of actual insight from the global stories
3. Final line: a question inviting a quick opinion
4. First comment: the macrosnaps.app link, posted immediately after publishing

**Sample post (March 17, 2026):**

> The same war is splitting the world's central banks in real time.
>
> Australia hiked to 4.10% today in a split 5-4 vote driven by oil-fueled inflation fears. The RBA governor warned a recession may be needed if prices stay sticky.
>
> On the same day, Canada's inflation collapsed to 1.8% and an April rate cut is virtually certain.
>
> Same geopolitical shock. Opposite policy responses. This is what 12 economies diverging looks like.
>
> Which central bank gets the call right — hike, hold, or cut?

## Launch Sequencing

1. Remove password gate, pick a soft-launch date
2. Announce on X with a 90-second Loom walkthrough
3. Post daily macro observations on X for 2 weeks before asking anyone to share or subscribe
4. Set up Substack and add footer CTA to macrosnaps.app
5. Run pipeline cleanly for 2-3 weeks, build early user base
6. Academic outreach (20-30 targeted emails)
7. Pitch niche newsletters (Ben's Bites, TLDR Finance, The Rundown)
8. Product Hunt launch
