Subject: The biggest oil shock in history just upended every 2026 forecast

The week in macro

Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz has created the largest oil supply disruption ever recorded, and financial markets are scrambling to reprice everything. What looked like a year of gentle rate cuts and cooling inflation three weeks ago now looks like a potential stagflation trap: growth slowing while energy costs surge. The 10-year Treasury yield jumping to 4.3% tells you traders have abandoned the "Fed cuts aggressively" playbook entirely.

What moved

Global energy markets

Brent at $102.75 represents a new regime, not a spike. With 20 million barrels per day blocked and Gulf producers running out of storage, this is a supply shock that strategic reserve releases can soften but not solve. The 60% surge in jet fuel prices is already filtering through to airline earnings guidance. Manufacturing activity is weakening as input costs climb. Every central bank that was preparing to cut rates is now staring at an inflation problem they thought they had solved.

United States

The Fed's path just got dramatically more complicated. Consensus had penciled in 100 to 125 basis points of cuts by year end, bringing rates toward 3.25%. That assumption is now in serious doubt. The labour market is softening (unemployment at 4.2%, February payrolls negative) which argues for easing, but energy driven inflation argues for holding. Goldman's 2.6% growth forecast already looked optimistic: they now estimate the oil shock could shave 0.3 to 0.5 percentage points if Brent stays above $90 through Q2. The fiscal picture offers no buffer, with the deficit running at 7.5% of GDP.

India

This is the economy most exposed to the Hormuz closure. Oil accounts for over a quarter of India's import bill, and a sustained price increase at current levels adds $20 to $30 billion annually to import costs. The current account deficit could widen from 1.2% to above 2.5% of GDP, triggering RBI intervention to support the rupee. Equities are already down 12.5% year to date, the worst performer in our coverage universe. The RBI's 6% policy rate was pointing toward cautious cuts: that easing cycle is now frozen.

Germany

The newly unlocked 500 billion euro fiscal package was supposed to revive German industry. Instead, Berlin is watching manufacturing margins compress further as energy costs spike, a cruel echo of the 2022 gas crisis. The DAX is down 8.8% year to date, reflecting how directly the Iran shock hits an industrial economy still adjusting to life without cheap Russian gas. The ECB faces a genuine dilemma: Germany's fiscal expansion creates inflationary pressure while the oil shock creates growth headwinds.

Brazil

The rare winner. Petrobras pre-salt production of 3.5 million barrels per day is highly profitable at $100 plus oil, generating windfall fiscal contributions. The Ibovespa is up nearly 10% year to date, dramatically outperforming peers. But the central bank cannot celebrate: the BCB's 12% policy rate reflects ongoing inflation concerns, and the risk of real depreciation pressure from global risk aversion means any easing cycle remains distant.

What to watch

  • FOMC communication over the coming week will be scrutinised for any shift in language around inflation expectations versus labour market conditions. A hawkish tilt would confirm markets' repricing of rate cut odds.
  • India's rupee is the canary in the emerging market coal mine. RBI reserve deployment and verbal intervention will signal how seriously policymakers view the balance of payments pressure.
  • European natural gas storage drawdown rates will indicate whether the oil shock is spreading to power markets, which would amplify the inflation impulse across the continent.
